Simple Suppressors gunshot effects

Eight sounds, one per caliber in the mod's own caliber system (matching
SimpleSuppressorsCompatibility's PART_CALIBERS/CALIBER_OPTIONS). Every gun
sharing a caliber shares its suppressor's sound: a suppressor barely touches
a supersonic bullet's crack (that is the bullet's own sonic boom, not muzzle
blast), so which specific firearm is chambered in that caliber does not
change what the suppressor itself sounds like.
  9mm      - handguns (Base.Pistol, and 9mm platforms from other mods)
  45acp    - handguns and pistol-caliber carbines (1911, TrapperCarbine, ...)
  44       - magnum handguns (Desert Eagle-class, .44 Magnum)
  357      - magnum handguns (.357 Magnum)
  556      - 5.56/.223 rifles, bolt-action or full-auto alike
  308      - .308/7.62x51 rifles
  3030     - .30-30 Winchester lever-action rifles
  shotgun  - 12 gauge

9mm and 45ACP: real recordings, used directly
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Real recorded suppressed gunfire of the exact caliber, trimmed to a single
shot and level-normalized only. No suppression is simulated -- the source
recordings already are of suppressed firearms.

44 and 357: real 45ACP crack + deep, boosted body layer
-----------------------------------------------------------------
No real suppressed recording of either magnum handgun caliber under a usable
open license was found. Both reuse the real 45ACP crack above with a real
unsuppressed-AR-15-derived low-frequency body layer added underneath,
lowpassed well below the crack's own energy (~260-320 Hz) and boosted 8-9 dB
above it so the magnum "thump" reads as clearly distinct from the lighter
9mm/45ACP handgun sounds, not just a marginally darker copy of them. .44
Magnum is cut slightly lower/heavier than .357 Magnum to reflect its larger
case and greater power.

Low-frequency body layer (44, 357, 556, 308, 3030)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A slice of a REAL unsuppressed CC0 5.56 AR-15 recording, lowpassed and
shaped to a natural decay, provides caliber-appropriate low-frequency
muzzle-blast mass under each real suppressed crack above. This is not a
second unsuppressed blast -- it is deliberately pushed to two clearly
distinct extremes rather than a subtle per-caliber nudge:
  - 308 (full-power rifle) and 44/357 (magnum handgun): heavily lowpassed
    (~260-320 Hz), long (200-260 ms), and boosted well above the crack's own
    level -- a genuinely deep, sustained "boom" tail.
  - 556 (intermediate rifle) and 3030 (lever-action, low-power rifle round):
    higher cutoff (~550-650 Hz), short (80-90 ms), and quiet relative to the
    crack -- a lean, crack-forward report with only a light body underneath.
This keeps mechanically similar guns of different power classes (a
full-auto 5.56 rifle vs a bolt-action .308) from converging on the same
sound just because they share a rifle-class crack recording.

Loudness mastering (applied to all eight sounds)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A single suppressed shot recording trimmed to a fixed window and
peak-normalized is not the same as a game-ready sound: measured, the raw
trims above integrated 19-23 dBFS quieter than their peak level implied,
because 80-95% of each file's duration is a near-silent phone-microphone
room-tone/reverb tail (real, but far below audibility, not a deliberate
"boominess"). That is what made every caliber read as muted in-game.
Every file is now: (1) trimmed to the last point its envelope is still
within 32 dB of its own peak, removing that dead air rather than any
audible content, then (2) compressed/limited (soft tanh knee, no hard
digital clipping) so its average loudness sits a fixed amount below its
peak instead of the 15-23 dB a raw single transient decay leaves. The
peak ceiling and that fixed amount are chosen per caliber by checking up to
4 overlapping instances (this mod's maxInstancesPerEmitter) at the
caliber's real maximum fire rate and picking the loudest combination that
still stays under 0 dBFS -- as loud as each caliber's fire rate allows
without the engine's overlapping copies summing into audible clipping.
9mm (600 rpm) and 5.56 (900 rpm) land quieter than the others for exactly
this reason, not because they are meant to sound weaker.

Shotgun
-----------------------------------------------------------------
No real suppressed-shotgun recording under a usable open license could be
found (suppressed shotguns are uncommon and rarely recorded/released). This
one remains derived from an unsuppressed recording:

Source: The Free Firearm Sound Library (see above)
Recording used: Nova/O_21P.wav, first close-recorded shot, onset 0.430 s.

The DSP that derives it is calibrated against the measured spectral profile
of the real recordings above (spectral centroid, high-frequency energy
ratio, crest factor) rather than guessed parameters: a light high-shelf
reduction and a short natural decay replace a hard lowpass and downward
compressor, which would otherwise read as an objectively muffled,
over-filtered signature next to real suppressed recordings (which measure
4.8-6.5 kHz centroid, 7-61% of energy above 5 kHz).

No audio from RSS or other Project Zomboid mods is included in this mod.
